What's included

Everything a finished basement needs, done in order.

A good basement starts with what's behind the walls and under the floor. We take care of the structure, moisture, and systems first, then the finishes.

01

Moisture & foundation

Signs of moisture and foundation cracks are checked and repaired before any walls go up, so the finished space stays dry. Foundation repair.

02

Support beams & openings

We move or replace support posts and beams and create new openings to open up the space, engineered and permitted.

03

Soffits & framing

Soffits built around ducts, pipes, and beams, plus new walls and insulation, for clean lines throughout.

04

Drain lines in the slab

We cut and dig through the concrete slab to run new drain lines, so a bathroom, kitchenette, or laundry can go where you want it.

05

Egress windows

We cut the opening in the foundation, dig the window well, and install a code-compliant egress window for a legal bedroom.

06

Plumbing & laundry

Bathrooms, kitchenettes, wet bars, and laundry rooms, with plumbing run and inspected under our license.

07

Electrical & lighting

New circuits, outlets, recessed lighting, and wiring for TVs, games, offices, and gyms.

08

Drywall, floors & finish

Drywall, ceilings, paint, and flooring suited to below-grade spaces, finished like the rest of the house.

FAQ

Basement questions.

Anything else, ask us at your consultation.

01Do I need a permit to finish my basement?

Finishing a basement usually requires permits, especially for framing, electrical, and plumbing. We coordinate permits as part of the project, before construction begins.

02What if my basement has moisture?

Moisture is the first thing we look at. Any signs of water are checked and addressed before walls and flooring go in, so the finished space stays dry.

03Can you add a bedroom in the basement?

Often, yes. A basement bedroom needs an egress window or door and has to meet other code requirements. We cut the opening, dig the window well, and install the egress window as part of the project.

04Can you move a support beam or post?

Often, yes. A structural engineer designs the new beam or post layout, we pull the permit, and we install temporary supports while the work is done.

05Can you add a bathroom, kitchenette, or laundry downstairs?

Yes. When there's no drain where you need one, we cut and dig through the concrete slab to run a new drain line, then patch the floor. We plan the plumbing during design so everything drains and vents properly.

06Are you licensed, insured, and bonded?

Yes. Julius Remodeling Group is a licensed, insured, and bonded Washington general contractor (License JULIURG780NJ). Verify it with Washington L&I.
